## How to use the list

You do not need to read every book. Identify one or two weak cells, choose a book for each, finish it and produce an output before choosing another. One book at a time.

Classics come first. They belong to more than one cell, but certain aspects stand out in each reading context.

## Dao · Understanding

### Classics shared by both paths

- **Dao De Jing:** Knowing others versus knowing yourself, overcoming others versus mastering yourself. A sustained exploration of direction beyond technique.
- **Analects:** Knowing the limits of knowledge, not becoming merely a specialized tool, following the heart without overstepping. Much of Confucius's concern is judgment rather than information.
- **The Great Learning and The Doctrine of the Mean:** Investigation, knowledge, sincerity and a rightly directed mind; emotions expressed in fitting measure. Sequences and standards for cultivation.
- **Wang Yangming, Instructions for Practical Living:** Unity of knowing and acting, practice in actual affairs, an unshaken center that responds to circumstances. Central to Chapters 11 and 12.
- **Marcus Aurelius, Meditations:** An emperor's self-examination, concerning steadiness and responsibility for consequences.
- **Epictetus, Enchiridion:** Distinguishing what is and is not within your control; a Stoic route to recognizing limits.

### Understanding on the thinking path

- **Nassim Nicholas Taleb, Antifragile and Skin in the Game:** Shared exposure to risk and why judgment without consequences can remain mere opinion.
- **Philip Tetlock, Superforecasting:** Foxes and hedgehogs, and why awareness of uncertainty can improve prediction.
- **Adam Grant, Think Again:** The psychology of moving from proving yourself right to discovering what is true.

### Understanding on the emotional path

- **Viktor Frankl, Man's Search for Meaning:** Human freedom in responding to circumstances.
- **Stephen Covey, The 7 Habits of Highly Effective People:** Inside-out development, emotional bank accounts, and understanding before seeking to be understood.

## Fa · Systems

### Systems for thinking

- **Ray Dalio, Principles:** Pain plus reflection and the extraction of personal principles from experience.
- **Anders Ericsson, Peak:** Why practice needs feedback.
- **Chris Argyris, work on organizational and double-loop learning:** The difference between reviewing a mistaken step and examining the assumptions that produced it.
- **Zeng Guofan, Family Letters and Twelve Daily Rules:** A Chinese example of plain, persistent systems for cultivation.

### Systems for emotional development

- **John Gottman, The Seven Principles for Making Marriage Work:** Positive and negative interactions, repair attempts, and relationships as ongoing actions.
- **Amy Edmondson, The Fearless Organization:** Psychological safety and the value of people being able to speak up.

## Shu · Techniques

### Thinking techniques

- **Daniel Kahneman, Thinking, Fast and Slow:** System 1 and System 2, base rates and the outside view; background for the pause discussed here.
- **Charlie Munger, Poor Charlie's Almanack:** Mental models, inversion and understanding opposing arguments.
- **Annie Duke, Thinking in Bets:** Separating decision quality from outcomes and speaking in probabilities.
- **Gary Klein, articles and interviews on premortems:** A low-cost technique for surfacing possible failure causes.
- **Richard Feynman, Surely You're Joking, Mr. Feynman!:** Intellectual honesty and the approach to understanding associated with explaining clearly.

### Interpersonal techniques

- **Marshall Rosenberg, Nonviolent Communication:** Observation, feeling, need and request; background for the needs list.
- **Daniel Goleman, Emotional Intelligence:** A familiar framework of self-awareness, self-regulation, empathy and social skills.
- **Douglas Stone and colleagues, Difficult Conversations:** Working with feelings as well as the substantive issue in important conversations.

## Qi · Tools

### Thinking tools

- **Atul Gawande, The Checklist Manifesto:** Why intelligence does not remove the need for checklists.
- **Tiago Forte, Building a Second Brain, or another useful personal-knowledge-management book:** External memory with a clear entry point, retrieval and regular review.

### Emotional tools

- **Lisa Feldman Barrett, How Emotions Are Made:** Emotional granularity and the value of precise labels.
- **Antonio Damasio, Descartes' Error:** The role of emotion in decision-making.
- **Carol Dweck, Mindset:** Replacing fixed identity claims with a view of abilities as developable.

## Notes on quotations

Classical passages in the Chinese original follow commonly used editions. Modern authors' ideas are paraphrased rather than reproduced verbatim. The English renderings here are explanatory translations, not quotations from a particular published English edition. Two attributions deserve special care:

- The saying about Dao without technique and technique without Dao is often assigned to the *Dao De Jing*, but is not a passage from it. Treat it as a later summary.
- The saying about a space between stimulus and response is often attributed to Frankl, but its precise source is uncertain; it is closer to Covey's presentation of Frankl's ideas.

These ideas are retained for their usefulness, while the attribution limits are acknowledged. This manual asks you to remain alert to what you think you know—including what this manual says.
