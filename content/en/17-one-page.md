## Two definitions

**Cleverness** solves problems: speed, range and skill in processing information.
**Wisdom** chooses problems and bears consequences: knowing what matters, recognizing ignorance and living with outcomes.

Cleverness brings techniques and tools to their peak. Wisdom begins with systems and understanding.

## One map

| | Thinking path | Emotional path |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Dao · Understanding** | From knowledge to judgment | Humanity, steadiness, clarity |
| **Fa · Systems** | Cognitive systems | Relationship and character systems |
| **Shu · Techniques** | Thinking skills | Interpersonal skills |
| **Qi · Tools** | Thinking toolkit | Emotional toolkit |

Share tools, practice techniques, establish systems, cultivate understanding. Learn from tools upward; act from understanding downward.

## Four stages

**Discover tools** → Stop collecting; use three tools for three months.
**Practice techniques** → One per month until it becomes natural.
**Establish systems** → Make good judgment less dependent on your mood.
**Cultivate understanding** → Teach, take responsibility, step back; return to tools as a student.

## Four imbalances

Strong technique, weak understanding: winning without living well.
Abstract understanding, unpracticed technique: fluent principles, hesitant action.
Many tools, little practice: collecting replaces training.
Rigid systems, lost purpose: the system runs while the person disappears.

## Seven signs of wisdom

1. Knowing what you do not know.
2. Holding opposing ideas while remaining able to act.
3. Taking a long view.
4. Not needing to win every time.
5. Handling complexity simply.
6. Bearing consequences.
7. More curiosity than certainty.

## Three conditions that nourish understanding

**Time**: shorten feedback loops. **Consequences**: take responsibility for the bill. **Reflection**: pain plus reflection can yield progress.

## Two selves

The self that wants to win speaks first. Judgment gives the self that wants to get it right a chance to speak.

## Three sequences for integration

Crucial conversations: feelings first, then the issue.
Major decisions: emotion as data, reason as an audit.
Failure: acceptance first, then review.

## Four turns

From proving yourself right to finding the truth.
From “I know” to “I may be wrong.”
From fast to right.
From beating others to surpassing yesterday's self.

## Daily practice: thirty minutes

**Three morning questions:** Today's most important judgment? Likely emotional trigger? The person I want to be?
**Name one emotion:** Which precise word fits, and which need does it represent?
**One decision-log entry:** What, why, expected result, confidence, review date.
**Read a passage:** Finish one book before starting another.
**Evening review:** Which judgment would I revise? Which interaction do I regret or appreciate? What did I learn?

## Three sayings

Know what you know and acknowledge what you do not.
Knowing others is intelligence; knowing yourself is clarity.
People must be honed through actual affairs before they can stand firmly.

## One sentence

The manual is not Dao. It is the finger pointing at the moon.
